## 1. Absa Group

### Industry
Banking and financial services

### What the company does
One of South Africa's big four banks, offering retail, business and corporate banking in South Africa and several other African markets. It runs a large youth employability programme alongside its banking business.

### Target Market
Individuals, SMEs and corporates. Its youth programmes target unemployed young people (mostly 18-30) and graduates.

### Products / Services
- Transactional accounts, credit, home loans, insurance and investments
- Absa ReadytoWork app (free work-readiness learning)
- Paid learnerships and apprenticeships (12 months, monthly stipend)
- Graduate debt-relief programme with the Feenix Trust

### Problem(s) They Solve
- Young people lack work-readiness, money-management and interpersonal skills when moving from education to work
- Graduates who owe historical fees cannot get their certificates, so they cannot prove their qualification to employers
- Lack of work experience blocks first jobs

### How They Currently Solve It
- ReadytoWork app teaches work-readiness, money management, entrepreneurship and soft skills; Absa reports more than 533,000 young people have benefited
- Committed more than R60 million to clear historical student debt, then links those graduates to jobs through the Youth Employment Service (YES)
- Runs 12-month learnerships with stipends for unemployed youth aged 18-30

### Technology / Innovation
Digital banking app plus the ReadytoWork app, which delivers employability training digitally rather than in classrooms.

### Relevant to Our Hackathon Problem?
- Yes - strongly relevant to skills verification and entry-level matching
- The withheld-certificate problem shows that a qualification you cannot prove is almost worthless to an employer
- Absa partners with YES and Feenix rather than building everything itself

### Interesting Ideas / Insights
- Proof of a skill can be blocked by things unrelated to the skill (debt, paperwork)
- Combining debt relief with job placement removes two barriers at once
- Banks see youth employment as part of financial inclusion strategy, not just CSI

### Potential Opportunities / Gaps
- It is unclear whether ReadytoWork completions produce anything an employer can verify - worth checking
- Opportunity: a verifiable digital record of skills and training that does not depend on a paper certificate
- Learnerships reach thousands; demand is in the millions

## 2. GoTyme Bank (formerly TymeBank)

### Industry
Digital banking / fintech

### What the company does
A South African digital bank that rebranded from TymeBank to GoTyme Bank in early 2026 to match the Tyme Group's international brand. It combines an app with physical access points, which it calls a "phygital" model.

### Target Market
Mass-market and underbanked South Africans, including people who have never held a formal bank account. It reports over 12 million customers in South Africa.

### Products / Services
- Transactional and savings accounts, money transfers, debit cards
- Kiosks in TFG and Boxer stores, plus new mall-based Customer Hubs (from March 2026)
- Cash deposits and withdrawals at retail tills, including Pick n Pay
- Upcoming banking partnership with Sanlam (soft launch November 2026)

### Problem(s) They Solve
- Traditional banking is costly and hard to access for low-income people
- Onboarding people who distrust or are unfamiliar with digital-only services

### How They Currently Solve It
- Customers can open an account, complete biometric KYC and get an activated card in under five minutes at a kiosk
- Kiosks were removed from Pick n Pay stores at the end of March 2026 because more customers now bank digitally; mall hubs replace them
- Uses retail partners as cash-in/cash-out points so customers do not need branches

### Technology / Innovation
Biometric identity verification, instant card issuing, mobile app, and a payments platform from BPC (SmartVista).

### Relevant to Our Hackathon Problem?
- Partly relevant - not a skills company, but it has solved a trust and verification problem at scale (identity)
- Its physical-plus-digital model is a direct answer to transport and data barriers

### Interesting Ideas / Insights
- Fast, trusted identity verification proves that verification can be quick if designed well
- Physical touchpoints build trust for digital-first products, especially for first-time users
- It changes its channel mix based on customer behaviour data

### Potential Opportunities / Gaps
- Opportunity: young people paid for micro-internships or gig work need a low-cost account to receive income - a possible partner
- Could the same kiosk-plus-app approach be used to verify skills in person?

## 3. MTN South Africa (MTN Group)

### Industry
Telecommunications

### What the company does
Mobile network operator across Africa. Beyond connectivity, it runs the MTN Skills Academy, a free online learning platform, which in August 2026 added an AI-powered Job Board.

### Target Market
Mobile users across 11 African markets where the Skills Academy operates; the Job Board targets young job seekers.

### Products / Services
- Mobile voice, data and fintech services
- MTN Skills Academy: courses in digital literacy, coding, data analytics, AI, cybersecurity and entrepreneurship
- AI Job Board: personalised job recommendations, skills-gap guidance and application tracking
- MTN Foundation ICT learnership

### Problem(s) They Solve
- Young people do not know which skills employers want or how close they are to a role
- Learning and job searching happen on separate platforms
- Data costs make job searching expensive for unemployed youth

### How They Currently Solve It
- The Job Board compares a learner's skills to job requirements, recommends roles by skills, location and goals, and suggests free courses to close gaps
- Users can save vacancies and track applications from submission to interview and offer
- The Skills Academy reports about 526,000 enrolments and 192,000 course completions since 2022

### Technology / Innovation
AI-based matching between learner profiles and vacancies; integrated learning, job discovery and tracking on one platform.

### Relevant to Our Hackathon Problem?
- Highly relevant - this is very close to our skills-to-jobs problem statement
- It is a direct competitor to anything we build around skills matching, so we must differentiate

### Interesting Ideas / Insights
- Roughly a third of enrolments end in a completion, which suggests drop-off is a real issue
- Course completion is not the same as proven ability - MTN matches on skills but it is unclear how those skills are verified
- Telcos are under regulatory pressure to zero-rate public-benefit sites; job platforms like SA Youth are already zero-rated on MTN

### Potential Opportunities / Gaps
- Verification: proving a learner can actually do the work, not just that they finished a course
- Practical experience: the Job Board recommends jobs but does not provide work experience
- Check whether the Skills Academy itself is zero-rated

## 4. Telkom (Telkom Group and Telkom Foundation)

### Industry
Telecommunications and ICT

### What the company does
South African telecoms and IT services group. Through the Telkom Foundation it funds education, ICT labs and youth digital skills programmes.

### Target Market
Consumers and businesses for telecoms; for skills programmes, unemployed youth, especially in rural and township communities, and school learners.

### Products / Services
- Fixed and mobile connectivity, IT services
- RT15 ICT Skills Learnership (NQF levels 4 and 5)
- Elevate Graduate Programme
- Telkom AI Institute (R100 million pledge, announced July 2026)
- ICT labs and devices for schools

### Problem(s) They Solve
- High youth unemployment - Telkom Foundation cites 45.8% for ages 15-34 in Q1 2026
- Shortage of digital and ICT skills, worst in rural and township areas

### How They Currently Solve It
- RT15 recruits 210 unemployed young people a year and gives them accredited qualifications; over R85 million invested and more than 1,100 learners trained
- The planned AI Institute will focus on practical, job-ready AI and digital skills
- Partners with provincial education departments to place ICT-trained youth in schools

### Technology / Innovation
ICT lab rollouts, connectivity infrastructure, partnerships with Microsoft and training providers for AI and cybersecurity courses.

### Relevant to Our Hackathon Problem?
- Relevant to skills development and practical experience
- Shows how corporates train youth but struggle to scale

### Interesting Ideas / Insights
- Accredited qualifications (NQF) are one way of making skills credible
- Programmes are tied to government and Treasury supplier-development initiatives
- Telkom places trained youth into real roles (school tech support), combining training with experience

### Potential Opportunities / Gaps
- 210 learners a year is tiny compared to the scale of youth unemployment
- Opportunity: a way for many more youth to prove ICT skills without a full 12-month learnership
- Little public detail on job outcomes after graduation

## 5. Sanlam

### Industry
Insurance and financial services

### What the company does
Africa's largest insurer, offering life and general insurance, investments and financial planning. In 2026 it is entering transactional banking through a partnership with GoTyme Bank.

### Target Market
Individuals and businesses across Africa and India, from mass market to wealthy clients.

### Products / Services
- Life, funeral and short-term insurance (including Santam)
- Investments and retirement products
- Retail credit (joint venture with GoTyme)
- Transactional banking via GoTyme - soft launch 1 November 2026, broader rollout in Q1 2027

### Problem(s) They Solve
- Customers need protection, savings and credit in one place
- Sanlam lacked everyday transactional banking for its clients

### How They Currently Solve It
- Instead of getting its own banking licence, Sanlam distributes banking products while GoTyme holds deposits and performs the regulated banking functions
- Expects operating losses of up to R550 million in 2026 while setting up the banking offering

### Technology / Innovation
Digital ecosystem combining credit, banking and loyalty rewards, built on GoTyme's banking platform.

### Relevant to Our Hackathon Problem?
- Low relevance to the skills problem statement
- High relevance to our finance concerns: it confirms that regulation makes building a bank from scratch very hard, even for a giant

### Interesting Ideas / Insights
- Even Africa's largest insurer chose partnership over building - a lesson for any finance idea we consider
- Insurers, banks and fintechs are converging (Old Mutual and Discovery also have banks)

### Potential Opportunities / Gaps
- If we pursue the finance statement, our idea should sit on top of licensed partners, not replace them
- Opportunity: financial products for young first-time earners (first salary, first savings)

## 6. Hollard

### Industry
Insurance

### What the company does
South Africa's largest privately owned insurance group, founded in 1980. It sells mostly through partners (retailers, brokers, underwriting managers) rather than direct sales.

### Target Market
South African consumers and businesses, plus other African markets (e.g. Ghana, Botswana) and Australia.

### Products / Services
- Life, funeral and short-term insurance
- Commercial and specialist insurance
- Embedded insurance sold through retail partners such as Edgars
- Micro-insurance through mobile and embedded channels

### Problem(s) They Solve
- Insurance is hard to access and understand for many South Africans
- Young people see insurance as irrelevant

### How They Currently Solve It
- Places insurance where people already shop - its partnership with Edgars has run for 25 years
- LNIG Hollard uses music partnerships and storytelling to reach younger customers
- Uses AI to improve underwriting, pricing and claims

### Technology / Innovation
AI in underwriting and claims, embedded distribution through partners' platforms.

### Relevant to Our Hackathon Problem?
- Low direct relevance to skills
- Useful as a model for distribution: reaching people through channels they already use

### Interesting Ideas / Insights
- Embedding a service into existing touchpoints beats asking people to come to you
- Cultural relevance (music, language) matters when targeting youth

### Potential Opportunities / Gaps
- Opportunity: embed skills profiles where young people already are (WhatsApp, retail, community spaces) rather than a new standalone app
- Possible future angle: income protection for gig and micro-internship workers

## 7. Allan Gray Orbis Foundation

### Industry
Philanthropy / education and entrepreneurship development

### What the company does
A foundation linked to the investment firm Allan Gray that identifies high-potential young people and develops them into entrepreneurs through university funding, mentorship and venture support.

### Target Market
High-achieving Grade 12 learners and university students in South Africa (also runs a programme in Namibia).

### Products / Services
- Allan Gray Fellowship (needs-based university funding)
- Personal industry mentors and an entrepreneurial curriculum
- Conferences, seminars and workshops
- Access to venture funding through its sister entity E Squared

### Problem(s) They Solve
- Talented young people lack funding, networks and support to become entrepreneurs
- South Africa needs more job creators, not just job seekers

### How They Currently Solve It
- Selects learners using academic criteria (e.g. 70% average, 60% maths) plus entrepreneurial potential
- Provides mentors and long-term development from school through university into business

### Technology / Innovation
Online entrepreneurial curriculum; otherwise mainly a people and programme model.

### Relevant to Our Hackathon Problem?
- Relevant to the mentorship and career-mobility focus areas

### Interesting Ideas / Insights
- Long-term mentorship is central to their model, not a one-off
- Very selective: one university intake offered only 10 fellowships across 11 partner universities
- They assess potential and character, not only marks

### Potential Opportunities / Gaps
- Strict academic criteria exclude most young people, including those with practical or informal skills
- Opportunity: scalable mentorship for youth who don't meet top academic thresholds

## 9. Clickatell

### Industry
Messaging and chat commerce (fintech / communications)

### What the company does
A South African-founded company (2000) that lets businesses communicate with and sell to customers inside chat channels such as WhatsApp, SMS, USSD and Apple Messages.

### Target Market
Businesses and banks that want to reach customers on mobile messaging, especially in emerging markets.

### Products / Services
- Chat Commerce Platform
- SMS API and gateway
- Chat-2-Pay with Visa (payments inside a chat)
- Chat Banking (KYC-compliant banking on WhatsApp)
- Chat Desk (agent inbox)

### Problem(s) They Solve
- Customers prefer chatting to downloading apps or visiting websites
- Businesses find it hard to take secure payments and do KYC in chat

### How They Currently Solve It
- Provides low/no-code tools for businesses to build chat journeys for marketing, support and payments
- Uses tokenised payments for secure transactions in WhatsApp
- In late 2025 announced a shift toward agentic commerce (AI agents completing tasks inside chats)

### Technology / Innovation
WhatsApp Business integration, USSD, card tokenisation, conversational AI.

### Relevant to Our Hackathon Problem?
- Relevant to data and access barriers - WhatsApp and USSD reach people without smartphones apps or much data

### Interesting Ideas / Insights
- WhatsApp is the default communication tool for many Africans
- USSD works on basic phones with no data at all
- One user review notes WhatsApp messaging is about three times the cost of SMS on the platform - cost matters at scale

### Potential Opportunities / Gaps
- Hackathon idea: deliver skills tasks, profiles or job alerts through WhatsApp/USSD instead of an app
- Check whether chat-based assessments could be harder to cheat with AI (live, timed interactions)

## 16. Harambee Youth Employment Accelerator

### Industry
Non-profit social enterprise / youth employment

### What the company does
A not-for-profit that connects first-time job seekers to employers. It runs SA Youth (sayouth.mobi), the national youth job platform under the Presidential Youth Employment Intervention.

### Target Market
Unemployed young South Africans (18-35) and employers hiring entry-level talent.

- SA Youth platform: jobs, learnerships, internships and training
- SA Youth Partner Network for employers
- Assessment and matching of young candidates
- Labour-market research and advocacy

- Job searching is costly (data, transport)

## 17. WeThinkCode_

### Industry
Education / software development training

### What the company does
A tuition-free South African coding academy (founded 2015) that trains young people from underserved communities to become work-ready software developers.

### Target Market
Young people aged 17-35, with no matric or coding experience required.

### Products / Services
- Software engineering learnership (NQF level 6), about 16 months of training plus paid internships
- Needs-based monthly stipend
- Peer-to-peer technical mentorship
- Campuses in Johannesburg, Cape Town and Durban, plus TVET college partnerships

### Problem(s) They Solve
- Shortage of software developers
- Talented youth are locked out of tech by fees and academic entry requirements

### How They Currently Solve It
- Selects students using online aptitude tests and a selection bootcamp instead of school results
- Project-based, peer-to-peer learning without formal lecturers
- Corporate sponsors fund tuition and offer internships
- Trains about 600 learners a year across seven campuses

### Technology / Innovation
Project-based learning with real industry tools; aptitude-based selection.

### Relevant to Our Hackathon Problem?
- Highly relevant - it already verifies potential through tests and builds proof through projects

### Interesting Ideas / Insights
- Selecting on aptitude rather than marks shows an alternative way to judge ability
- Sponsors trust graduates because they see project work and internship performance
- Former employees mention that job placement after the programme is not always clear

### Potential Opportunities / Gaps
- Capacity of about 600 per year is small compared to demand
- Full-time campus attendance creates transport and living-cost barriers
- Opportunity: a lighter, remote way to prove coding ability for those who can't attend full-time
